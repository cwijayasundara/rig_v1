# Codebase design (adapted from Matt Pocock's `codebase-design`, MIT, pointed at rig's artifacts)
This skill is a reference, not a stage: it adds no gate, approval or artifact of its own. Use these terms exactly; do not substitute "component", "service", "API" or "boundary" - consistent language is the point. **Module**: anything with an interface and an implementation, at any scale (function, class, package, tier-spanning slice). **Interface**: everything a caller must know to use the module correctly - signature plus invariants, ordering, error modes, required configuration and performance characteristics. **Implementation**: what is inside the module; distinct from adapter (a thing can be a small adapter with a large implementation, or a large adapter with a small implementation). **Depth**: leverage at the interface - behaviour a caller or test can exercise per unit of interface learned; deep means much behaviour behind a small interface, shallow means the interface is nearly as complex as the implementation. **Seam**: a place where behaviour can be altered without editing in that place; where a module's interface lives, and placing it is its own design decision. **Adapter**: a concrete thing that satisfies an interface at a seam; it names a role, not a substance. **Leverage**: what callers get from depth - one implementation paying back across many call sites and tests. **Locality**: what maintainers get from depth - change, bugs and knowledge concentrate in one place instead of spreading across callers.
Principles: 1. **Deep over shallow** - can I reduce the methods, simplify the parameters, hide more complexity inside? 2. **The interface is the test surface** - callers and tests cross the same seam; if a test must reach inside, the seam is in the wrong place. 3. **Deletion test** - would deleting a suspected-shallow module concentrate complexity (good) or merely move it (pass-through; cut it)? 4. **One adapter is a hypothetical seam, two are a real one** - no interface for a single implementation on speculation; this is rig's "no abstraction with a single implementation" rule stated positively. 5. **Place seams where behaviour varies** - inject at the boundary and keep domain logic free of frameworks, I/O and the clock (guides/engineering.md), so the deep part stays testable through its interface. In rig: use this vocabulary in `design.md`/`plan.md` `## Approach` and `## Design` - name each new module's interface and seam, and why it is deep enough to earn its existence. Respect `.rig/product/architecture.md` and the ADRs in `.rig/product/adr/`: contradicting an accepted ADR is a `## Concerns` bullet for its owner, not a silent override. In review, a shallow module, a leaked seam or an interface wider than its implementation is a design finding in these terms at the usual confidence bar, never a mechanical sensor. For test seams follow guides/testing.md: test behaviour through the interface, fake only at the boundary. When invoked directly, reply in at most 15 lines: the question restated in this vocabulary, the seam you recommend, and the depth trade-off.
