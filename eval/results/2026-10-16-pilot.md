## Pilot findings (not a lift measurement)

The pilot ran 1 run per arm to prove the live path. It did: all five arms ran live end to end for $1.73 in total (estimate was $10–30). The scores do **not** measure rig's lift, for three reasons in the eval setup, not the models:

1. **The brief is vaguer than the hidden acceptance.** 4 of 5 arms answered `GET /products` with `{"products": [...]}` where the acceptance expects a bare array, and 2 arms accepted `tea` as a SKU: the brief states neither the response shapes nor the SKU format. Every arm passed `npm test` (its own tests) and failed both acceptances on these points.
2. **The final-policy arm stopped at approval.** The model left open questions in its spec; the runner plays the person but cannot answer them, so the run ended before any build.
3. **Permission denials cost every arm turns.** 27 denials across the 5 runs: compound shell commands and heredocs (all arms), writes outside the repo (bare arms), and, in the rig arms, skill-to-skill calls (`/rig:test` from inside a build), long-line searches prompted by the size limit, and `gh pr comment` outside the pr skill.

Fix these, then run the pilot again before a full pass. At pilot prices a full pass (50 runs) is roughly $20, not $100–300.
