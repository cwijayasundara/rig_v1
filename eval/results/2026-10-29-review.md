## Notes on this run (verification evidence, item 1)

- 72 calls (12 cases x 2 models x 3 runs, clean arm only), no failures, $5.61: Sonnet 5.5 $1.82, Opus 5.5 $3.79.
- **Gate not met.** The proposal's gate was hard recall below 100% for at least one reviewer. Both models caught every
  hard case in every run, so the eval still cannot rank reviewers. It decides nothing about Sonnet against Opus or one
  reviewer against finders + verifier.
- **Precision held.** 0 findings on the three clean controls in 9 runs each, the two baited ones included: on this brief,
  neither model raised findings against hypothetical concerns. Extra findings on defect runs: 0.52 (Opus), 0.56 (Sonnet).
- **Catches were not read.** Rows kept counts only, and the patterns are loose (any `cache` finding on `cart.js` counts
  for lines-cache-stale), so some catches may be a different finding that happens to match. Rows now keep the review
  text (`text`) so the next run can be audited.
- **Why the hard cases were easy:** the whole source tree is about 100 lines and sits in the prompt; set-qty-edges' spec
  names the qty-0 edge outright; every defect breaks a numbered B-line the reviewer is told to check. Difficulty here
  came from the kind of defect, but a reviewer's misses in practice come from scale and from defects no spec line names.
- **What would discriminate:** a real codebase in the thousands of lines (shop_app, or this repo's own `scripts/`); a
  defect inside a multi-file diff of a few hundred lines; defects no B-line names (money rounding, an error path, a
  race in `server.js`); and real reverts mined from shop_app history.
- Equal recall at half the price is a hint for Sonnet on small, spec-explicit diffs, not evidence: the eval is
  saturated, so it cannot show where the two differ.
