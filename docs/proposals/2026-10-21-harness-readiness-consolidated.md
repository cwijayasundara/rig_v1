# Earn parallelism: rig's path to production

## Verdict

rig should become production-ready by earning trust before it offers scale, not by adding capability. The sources converge on one stack: make the bad pattern impossible in the architecture, hard-fail what remains in CI, keep prose guidance for judgment calls only, and promote every repeated correction up that ladder after proving the new check fails on a real past mistake. They also agree that agents must verify against the running app, that writes stay single-threaded with a clean-context diff-only reviewer, that humans own constraints and the review bottleneck, and that scaffolding must be removed when models outgrow it. rig already has the process half (person-only approval, frozen contracts, base-branch CI judge) and has left the structural half switched off: the `layering` sensor is inert by default, linter configs are never generated, and there is no Maintain stage (`research_notes/Rig harness production readiness/rig_audit.md`). The binding constraint is evidence: rig's only paired eval accepted 0/1 on every arm, and every external number below is a vendor or self-report. This report therefore supersedes the earlier proposal with a five-phase plan that starts by measuring and fixing false greens, keeps the earlier P0/P1 core, adds real-app verification, a clean-context review pattern and CI-injection hardening, and defers parallelism and org features until an honest eval shows lift. The poteto talk itself was not read (secondary summaries only), so nothing here depends on its exact wording.

## The consolidated model: seven principles that recur

**Confidence key:** primary-read (page fetched and read), secondary (write-ups or search snippets), vendor self-report (operator's own numbers), unverified (single secondary source).

**1. Trust before parallelism.** poteto's thesis is that "you can't spawn 100 agents when you don't even trust the output of one": she supervised 1-5 conversations until verification existed, and opening 100 agents early produced "garbage PRs, regressions, and bugs" ([redreamality](https://redreamality.com/blog/lauren-tan-poteto-trust-before-parallel/); secondary, quote via [mvanhorn](https://api.fxtwitter.com/mvanhorn/status/2111481455906103390)). Cognition independently reports that parallel writer swarms still see little adoption and that large demos worked only because success criteria were simple and verifiable ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working); vendor self-report). Anthropic's agent-teams docs warn of "significantly more tokens" and poor fit for sequential or same-file work ([docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams); primary-read). Parallelism is also bounded by CI latency and merge-queue design before model capacity ([Graphite](https://graphite.com/blog/the-first-stack-aware-merge-queue); vendor model, snippet).

**2. A guardrail ladder, strongest first.** Architecture that makes the bad pattern impossible, then static analysis and CI hard-fail, then rules, bots and skills (guidance agents may skip), then style guides and human review ([redreamality](https://redreamality.com/blog/lauren-tan-poteto-trust-before-parallel/); secondary; the order of the top four is consistent across summaries, verification's placement varies). Böckeler's sensors experiment supports the empirical core: dependency-cruiser layering was violated a few times and then self-corrected, while agents often skipped sensors unless prompted and AGENTS.md was unreliable for triggering them ([martinfowler.com](https://martinfowler.com/articles/sensors-for-coding-agents.html); primary-read). Cursor says "constraints beat instructions" ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases); self-report). The 2026 harness survey finds behavioural policy migrating from prompt prose to configuration ([arXiv 2609.00006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00006); abstract only).

**3. Every correction is promoted up the ladder and proven against a real past mistake.** pstack's `/correct` fixes at the highest level (architecture, then types/lints/CI, then tests, docs last "only for judgment calls"), requires each new check to fail on a real past mistake, puts exceptions on the offending line with reason, expiry and human approval, and counts a class once it has happened twice ([correct SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cursor/plugins/main/pstack/skills/correct/SKILL.md); primary-read). Cursor's Bugbot adds the demotion half: candidate rules from reactions and replies are promoted as signal accumulates and disabled when they stop helping ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/changelog/04-08-26); self-report). Böckeler's open question, whether a sensor that never fires means quality or blindness, is answered by the prove-it-fails rule.

**4. Verification drives the real app.** poteto's "control glass" is a project-local CLI that drives the app via Chrome DevTools Protocol plus a maintained "feature map" of how features are reached; neither works alone, and it proves behaviour, not code quality ([redreamality](https://redreamality.com/blog/lauren-tan-poteto-trust-before-parallel/); secondary). The public analogue is pstack's `/create-verification-skill`: "A generated skill that was never executed is a draft, not a deliverable" ([SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cursor/plugins/main/pstack/skills/create-verification-skill/SKILL.md); primary-read). Anthropic found unit tests and `curl` missed end-to-end failures that browser testing caught ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents); primary-read). Cognition admits models run JavaScript to trigger states instead of clicking and that UI-only enforcement is "in progress" ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/testing-development)). Reward-hacking papers show green tests are weak evidence (titles and snippets only: [SpecBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21384)).

**5. Single-threaded writes, clean-context diff-only review.** Cognition's reviewer works best with no shared context (only the diff), and the original author filters findings against the user's instructions; they report about 2 bugs per Devin PR, roughly 58% severe, mostly fixed before a human looks ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working); vendor self-report). Anthropic: out of the box Claude is a weak QA agent that flags real issues then approves anyway, and separating worker from judge is "a strong lever" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps); quote via [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/04/anthropic-three-agent-harness-ai/)). pstack's `poto-mode` agrees: every subagent diff is reviewed, follow-ups go to a fresh subagent, nothing lands before an independent verdict, and "Green is not safe" ([SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cursor/plugins/main/pstack/skills/poteto-mode/SKILL.md); primary-read).

**6. Humans own constraints and review the bottleneck.** poteto's humans move from supervising conversations to tending layers; human review is used to find gaps, not to scale ([redreamality](https://redreamality.com/blog/lauren-tan-poteto-trust-before-parallel/)). Devin states review, not generation, is the bottleneck ([Devin Review](https://cognition.com/blog/devin-review)); OpenAI staff say the same ([LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/ai/openai-says-there-are-easily-1000x-engineers-now); self-report). Stripe humans review every PR ([Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents); self-report). The exception is Lopopolo, who says most human review happens post-merge and tells review agents to bias toward merging and ignore findings below P2 ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/p/harness-eng); primary-read interview), a posture that depends on one-minute builds, a land skill and disposable code. Faros telemetry shows the cost of ignoring this: high-AI teams merged about 98% more PRs but review time rose 91% and org-level DORA metrics did not improve ([softwareseni](https://www.softwareseni.com/the-ai-productivity-paradox-in-software-development-why-developers-feel-faster-but-measure-slower/); vendor, secondary).

**7. Remove scaffolding when models outgrow it, one component at a time; and secure pipelines that read untrusted text.** Anthropic dropped context resets ("dead weight" with Opus 4.5), sprint decomposition and per-sprint QA, kept the planner and one end-of-run evaluator, and found the method was removing one component at a time because a radical cut failed ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps); primary-read, self-report). SkillsBench finds curated skills help (about +16 points) while self-generated skills give no average benefit ([arXiv 2602.12670](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670v1); snippet). On the security side, Clinejection (a malicious issue title led to credential theft and a tampered package) and "Comment and Control" (one PR comment made Copilot, Claude Code or Gemini agents leak tokens) are documented 2026 cases ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-github-actions-security-20260503-csa-st/); snippet). Devin's Auto-Triage treats inbound text as untrusted and sandboxes the network ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/auto-triage)); Anthropic stores credentials in a vault behind a proxy rather than trusting token scoping ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents)).

## Gap analysis against rig

The audit shows rig strong where sources are weakest and weak where they are strongest. Against principle 2: `scripts/registry.ts` lists the built-in `layering` sensor but no pack or `init` populates `options.layers`, `architecture.md` is prose nothing reads, and pack linters (dependency-cruiser, ArchUnit, import-linter) run only "if config exists" and are never generated (`templates/stacks/*.json`). Against principle 3: `/rig:rule` and `learn mine` (`scripts/learn*.ts`) have a replay gate and a human apply step, but no threshold, no "proven against a past mistake" record, and no exception expiry; waivers are free text with no owner or age. Against principle 4: `/rig:test` writes `verify.json` tied to a tree stamp (`scripts/lifecycle.ts`), and `tests/e2e/fixtures/` holds reference apps, but the audit found no generated `verify-<app>` skill, feature map or UI-only drive in the target repo; the live e2e driver has not run since the rebuild. Against principle 5: review is native `/code-review` inside `/rig:pr` (`skills/pr`); whether it runs diff-only in a clean context is not recorded, and no reviewer calibration metric exists. Against principle 6: no `rig report` of review time, PR size, first-pass CI rate or repeat-mistake rate. Against principle 7: no component is tagged with the model weakness it compensates for, no ablation runs at model release (`eval/run.mjs` supports paired arms but only one pilot exists), and no audit of `templates/rig-check.yml` for least privilege has been recorded. Drift GC (Maintain) is not started: no scan script, no scheduled routine, nightly cadence has no runner. Context output is CLAUDE.md only, with no AGENTS.md. The repo's own status records also conflict (wiki "built" in README but unmerged on `rig/wiki` per memory; `hooks/dashboard.tsx` exists though GOAL.md calls the journey "not started"); reconcile before relying on them.

Rules applied to every item: native first, deterministic outside the model, no pruning without evidence, mods not hooks, thin layer, eval before keeping, two human stops unchanged, tiers and budgets stay Non-Goals. Sizes are rough and unverified against the 6,000-line harness cap.

## Junior and senior operating model

The sources describe mechanisms, not experience tiers, and none reports junior outcomes. Stripe and Spotify make launching trivial while verification is non-bypassable ([Stripe](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents)); Cognition's cloud sessions inherit the dispatching engineer's permissions with an audit trail, so a junior's agent cannot exceed the junior's access ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/what-we-learned-building-cloud-agents)); seniors write playbooks once and code owners verify across repos ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/devin-annual-performance-review-2025)); poteto notes Dune suits non-engineers submitting changes (secondary). For rig this means seniors author the constraint layer (layer block, exemplars, rules, promotion approvals, playbook-style intents with postconditions and forbidden actions, which Cognition recommends) and review a sample of merged work to find where the team struggles. Juniors take slice-scoped intents and run `/rig:start` through `/rig:build`; the scope check (1.3), generated linters (1.1) and real-app verification (2.1) make mistakes fail mechanically. Differentiation comes from CODEOWNERS and independent GitHub approval on `.rig/**` and layer changes (4.3), not a junior mode, which would breach the tiers Non-Goal.

The learning risk is real. Anthropic's randomised trial (about 52 developers learning an unfamiliar Python library) found the AI group scored 17% lower on a comprehension quiz with no significant speed gain, and developers who asked conceptual questions scored about 65% or more versus under 40% for those delegating code generation ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills); figures via [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/02/ai-coding-skill-formation/), paper not opened; small sample, new-library task). Osmani's comprehension-debt framing is opinion built on that one trial ([addyosmani.com](https://addyosmani.com/blog/comprehension-debt/)). A candidate mechanism, untested, is that the plan-approval step shows a plain-language summary and asks the approver to state one risk before approving; test it against review quality before keeping. Amazon reportedly required senior sign-off for AI-assisted production changes by junior and mid-level engineers after two outages, but this rests on anonymous FT sourcing relayed by aggregators, Amazon disputes the AI cause and blames misconfigured access (the agent inherited operator permissions) ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/amazon-is-making-even-senior-engineers-get-code-signed-off-following-multiple-recent-outages)). Treat it as weak evidence for permission scoping, not for seniority gates. Factory's readiness score is the one productised precedent for helping a mixed team: it makes "is this repo safe for agents" a repo property any engineer can read, with level 3 called production-ready and share of repos at L3+ as the KPI ([Factory](https://factory.com/news/agent-readiness); self-report). That is 4.1.

## What not to build, risks, and open decisions

Do not build an orchestrator, a Dune equivalent, a company-brain, a tier system, spend ledgers, a second review or extra human stops, an LLM scope judge before the deterministic check shows a gap, or Specmatic-dependent features while Java and the licence block. Do not copy post-merge-only review. Do not build a self-generated skills library; SkillsBench found no average benefit. Do not use PR count as a target: poteto's own number is an activity metric, likely includes many small automated PRs, and no defect, revert, review-time or cost data accompanies it.

Risks: every external number is a self-report; the harness effect is never isolated; Cursor, Cognition and Anthropic results are on greenfield or demo work; the rig base is unproven at n=1; sensors can push complexity elsewhere (Böckeler saw overly tight max-lines rules do this); verification skills inherit Cognition's UI-realism failures; layer rules can go stale as models change, so each generated rule needs an owner and a retirement eval; the 6,000-line cap may bind; Windows is unsupported.

Open decisions for the maintainer: (1) whether to accept the CODEOWNERS-only stance on junior and senior, or reopen the tiers Non-Goal if an eval shows unscoped juniors cost more per accepted task; (2) the accepted-rate floor that unlocks Phase 3 parallelism; (3) whether `rig readiness` is repo-level reporting (compatible) or edges toward a tier; (4) whether AGENTS.md is worth emitting before verifying native Claude Code support; (5) promotion threshold, two (pstack) or three (one blog), best settled by the 0.2 eval; (6) whether the review subagent should be diff-only by default; (7) reconcile the wiki and dashboard status contradictions; (8) whether waivers require owner and expiry.

## Corrections to the earlier report

First, the earlier report presented "a PR comment typed more than 3 times becomes a lint rule" as Dune's rule; it appears only in the HappyRock blog, no talk summary confirms it, and pstack's primary `/correct` says twice. Second, it called the "5-10 PRs per engineer per day after GPT-5.2" and "human sampling after merge" claims unverified; both are in the Latent Space interview (primary-read), though neither is in the OpenAI post, and the interview says most human review happens post-merge. Third, poteto's "1,000+ PRs per month" is superseded: the title says 2,500, the talk about 2,000, a profile article cites about 1,000 in July and 2,000 in August; all self-reported. Fourth, the earlier "three guardrail layers" is a simplification; summaries give four or five layers with consistent top-four order. Fifth, the earlier report relied on Böckeler's February memo; her April 2026 article supersedes it and her May piece adds hands-on findings (agents skip sensors; mutation testing found 13 surviving mutants at 100% statement coverage). Sixth, "Cursor and Anthropic reportedly say guardrails go stale" is now primary for Anthropic (harness-design post) and self-report for Cursor. Seventh, the earlier "20+ parallel agents" and "20 agents" style figures for poteto are not in any talk summary, and the talk argues against numbers-first scaling. Eighth, blind skill evals judged by another model family and five scoped-memory specialist bots are single-source (HappyRock), not confirmed. Ninth, the earlier report had no Cognition, Factory or Anthropic-harness evidence on clean-context review and readiness scoring; those are new here.

## Conclusion

The most useful novelty is that the sources disagree less about what to build than about when: poteto, Cognition and Anthropic all put trust and verification ahead of scale, and the loudest scale claims (Lopopolo, Cursor) come with the strongest caveats about greenfield work, fast builds and cheap rollback. For rig that turns the sequence into a rule: no new capability merges until an honest eval shows it earns its dollars, and every guardrail ships with a way to retire it. Seniority then shows up as authorship of constraints and the promotion of corrections, and a junior's safety comes from checks that fail loudly, which is a claim the evidence supports only by design argument and vendor report until rig measures it.
