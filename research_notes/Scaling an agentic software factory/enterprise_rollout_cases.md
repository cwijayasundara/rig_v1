# Enterprise rollouts of AI coding agents (as of Nov 2026): consultancies and product companies

Method note: ~17 searches/fetches. Primary pages fetched in full: Spotify engineering blog, InfoQ (Spotify), Pragmatic Engineer (Uber, partly paywalled), Business Today (Indian IT), arXiv abstract (Microsoft). Everything else is from search-result summaries and was NOT opened in full; those are flagged "snippet-only". Vendor press releases are marketing for adoption claims.

## What have the big consultancies/SIs publicly done?

### Takeaway
Consultancy announcements are overwhelmingly "up to N seats / N trained" partnership press releases with no phase data or outcome metrics. The only measured outcomes come from Microsoft M365 Copilot usage at Indian IT firms (general knowledge work, not coding agents) and from product companies. Accenture, Cognizant, Deloitte, Infosys, TCS and Wipro all announced Anthropic tie-ups with Centers of Excellence and training targets.

Announced eight days after a separate Accenture/OpenAI collaboration (vendor-neutral hedging). ### Inferences
- Common template across Accenture/Deloitte/Cognizant/Infosys: (1) vendor partnership, (2) dedicated Center of Excellence or business group, (3) a certified/trained cohort an order of magnitude smaller than headcount (Accenture ~30k of ~770k is my arithmetic from outside knowledge; Deloitte 15k of 470k, Wipro 10k, TCS 50k), (4) internal use first to build client playbooks. For a 33,000-engineer firm, a trained/certified core of ~3-10% of engineers is the market norm.
- Seat count announcements ("up to 350,000") should not be read as active coding-agent users.

### Gaps
- No public phase/gate data, active-user rates, or measured delivery outcomes for Claude Code at any consultancy. Found nothing on PwC, KPMG, EY, IBM Consulting, Thoughtworks internal rollouts, nor Capgemini/HCLTech coding-agent seats. Primary Infosys/TCS/Wipro press releases not opened.

## What have large product companies published about scaled rollouts and background agents?

### Takeaway
The best-documented cases are Spotify (Honk + Fleet Management), Uber (multi-tool, internal Minion/Shepherd, budget blowout), Stripe (Minions), and Microsoft (academic study). The winners had years of prior platform standardisation, and all report verification/review as the new bottleneck.

### Cited Findings
- Spotify (first-party, fetched): >99% of engineers use AI coding tools weekly, 94% report more productive (self-report); PRs per engineer up 76%; monthly merged changes ~8,100 to 17,000 YoY (Aug); >2.5M automated maintenance PRs merged via Fleet Management, mostly auto-merged after checks; a Java migration across backend services took three days. Honk = background coding agent running Claude via the Claude Agent SDK in Kubernetes pods; Fleetshift does targeting/scheduling/tracking; adoption "accelerated sharply with Opus 4.5"; Honk later added to Slack, v2 with shared sessions and team projects. Platform team owns Fleetshift, Honk and Backstage. Earlier post (Nov 2025): >1,500 AI PRs merged, ~half of Spotify PRs automated since mid-2024 — [Spotify Eng 2026](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/6/code-with-claude-coding-is-no-longer-the-constraint), [Spotify Eng 2025](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2025/11/spotifys-background-coding-agent-part-1), [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/10/spotify-ai-quality-controls)
- Stripe Minions: one-shot, end-to-end agents built on a fork of Block's Goose; internal MCP server "Toolshed" with ~500 tools; max two CI rounds; started from Slack/bugs/feature requests. Stripe's own post (Feb 2026) says >1,000 merged PRs/week fully minion-produced, human-reviewed; ~1,300/week in later secondary reports — [Stripe Dev blog](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents), [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/03/stripe-autonomous-coding-agents), [Port.io](https://software-factories.port.io/stripe). (Stripe post itself not opened by me; details from search summary.)
- Uber (Pragmatic Engineer, Mar 2026, fetched): 84% of devs are "agentic coding users", 92% use agents monthly; 11% of PRs opened by agents; Claude Code usage 32% (Dec) to 63% (Feb) while IDE tools (Cursor, IntelliJ) plateaued; AI-related costs up 6x since 2024. Internal stack: Minion (background agents), Shepherd (migrations), uReview (AI review), Code Inbox, Autocover (5,000+ tests/month), MCP Gateway, AIFX CLI. A temporary "tiger team" designed MCP strategy; the DevEx platform team built the CLI. Lessons: adoption slower than expected; top-down mandates less effective than peer-shared wins; review load and noise up; parallel agents create cost pressure — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-uber-uses-ai-for-development)
- Goldman Sachs: deployed Cognition's Devin in 2025 alongside ~12,000 human developers ("hundreds" of agents), expanded to Claude in 2026; the 3-4x productivity figure is an expectation from a secondary source, not audited — [Lucidate](https://lucidate.substack.com/p/goldman-sachs-scales-ai-coding-to)
- Microsoft (academic, authors Murphy-Hill, Butler, Savelieva, submitted Jul 1 2026): study of "tens of thousands" of engineers in early-2026 Claude Code + GitHub Copilot CLI rollout; adopters merged ~24% more PRs than counterfactual, persisting over the four-month window; first use spread mainly through social networks (skip-level peer use strongest per secondary summaries); authors say organisations should treat visible peer use as central to rollout strategy and note token spend can reach millions of dollars annually — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01418)
- Amazon Q/Kiro: one unverified secondary report claims a Kiro agent deleted a production environment in Feb 2026 causing ~13-hour outage — unconfirmed — [search summary, see Gaps]

### Inferences
- "Software factory" success cases share: central platform team owning the agent harness, deterministic guardrails (CI cap, linting, rollback), tool access via MCP gateway, and narrow task classes (migrations, dependency upgrades, small bugs) first.
- Claude Code share rising fastest at Uber and Spotify suggests CLI/background agents displace IDE assistants in mature orgs.

### Gaps
- No usable material found for Shopify, Salesforce, Atlassian, Booking, JPMorgan coding-agent rollouts, Ramp Inspect, Google internal, Cursor/Codex/Devin enterprise case studies with numbers. Stripe/Uber primary posts partially unread.

## How were rollouts phased (pilot size, cohorts, champions, gates)?

### Takeaway
Little hard phase data is public. The strongest evidence says peer-driven diffusion beats mandates, and that platform foundations precede agents. Anthropic/Deloitte/Accenture phases are described only as "train, CoE, internal-first, then clients".

### Cited Findings
- Uber: mandates less effective than engineers sharing wins; adoption slower than expected; tiger team for MCP, platform team for CLI; workflow moved single-agent, to parallel agents — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-uber-uses-ai-for-development)
- Microsoft: social-network spread, skip-level peers most influential — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01418)
- Spotify: automation foundation (Fleet Management) years before agents; deterministic scripts failed on complex changes, then LLM agent; adoption jump with a model release — [Spotify Eng](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/6/code-with-claude-coding-is-no-longer-the-constraint)
- Infosys: internal use in one engineering org first; client rollout starts with one vertical (telecom) before five domains — [CRN Asia](https://www.crnasia.com/india/news/2026/infosys-partners-anthropic-to-integrate-claude-into-enterprise-ai-deployments)
- Infosys Copilot: "next phase enterprise-wide scaling" requires leadership alignment, workforce readiness and governance — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/artificial-intelligence/story/from-ai-pilots-to-everyday-work-what-are-indias-it-giants-doing-differently-553336-2026-09-04)

### Inferences
- A pilot-first design for a 33k-engineer firm should seed visible champions in each cohort and measure retention, not just first use; gates should be quality/incident metrics, not usage.

### Gaps
- No source gave pilot sizes, durations per phase, or formal gate criteria at any company. Not found.

## Which measured results are credible, and which are marketing?

### Takeaway
Credible: Microsoft's ~24% merged-PR lift (peer-reviewed-style counterfactual, merged PRs only), Spotify's PR/change counts (first-party but self-reported), Faros telemetry (vendor but large). Weak: vendor "up to" seat counts, productivity percentages in press releases, self-reported "feels faster" surveys. Organisational delivery metrics often do not move.

### Cited Findings
- Faros 2025: >10,000 devs, 1,255 teams; high-adoption devs completed 21% more tasks, merged 98% more PRs; PR review time +91%; org-level DORA metrics did not improve proportionally (vendor with interest in measurement) — [Faros](https://www.faros.ai/research/ai-acceleration-whiplash) via search summary
- Faros 2026 "Acceleration Whiplash": 22,000 devs, 4,000+ teams, two years; probability of production incident per merged change more than tripled (single-vendor claim) — [Faros blog](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways)
- METR 2025 RCT: experienced devs ~19% slower; METR 2026 follow-up inconclusive (CIs include zero; control-group problems) — secondary sources only, [ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/measure-ai-coding-agent-productivity-dora-cloudwatch-2026/)
- DORA 2025: AI adoption linked to higher throughput but lower delivery stability; seven foundational capabilities; a 2026 DORA piece reportedly describes a J-curve — secondary — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/10/spotify-ai-quality-controls), [Augment](https://www.augmentcode.com/guides/ai-productivity-paradox-engineering-delivery)
- Spotify: PRs +76%; "no matching rise in rework" (self-reported) — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/10/spotify-ai-quality-controls)
- Uber cost: AI costs 6x since 2024 — [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/how-uber-uses-ai-for-development)

### Inferences
- Treat vendor adoption stats ("86% daily", "99% weekly") as activity not impact. Use merged-PR counterfactuals plus incident/rework/review-time as the evidence bar.

### Gaps
- No credible cost-per-engineer figures beyond Uber's cap (below). Did not retrieve METR or DORA primaries.

## Documented failures, reversals and lessons

### Takeaway
Failures cluster in three areas: cost blowouts (Uber), verification/review capacity (Spotify, Faros, Uber), and incident risk from automated changes. Reputational AI-quality failures at consultancies (Deloitte Australia refund) are a related but non-coding precedent.

### Cited Findings
- Uber cost blowout: per The Information (via secondary reports), Uber exhausted its entire 2026 AI coding budget in roughly four months after Claude Code took off, then set a $1,500/month per-employee cap for agentic tools (exceptions by request); heavy users reportedly up to $2,000/month — [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/uber-2026-ai-budget-claude-code-token-spend), [CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/ubers-finance-team-overtaken-engineering-ai-use/821513). Rollout of Claude Code + Cursor began Dec 2025 — secondary sources; The Information original not accessed. Other Uber stats in secondary press (95% adoption, 70% AI-origin code) conflict with Pragmatic Engineer's 84%/92%.
- Spotify incident (June 24, 2026): content-ingestion delay of hours caused by scheduled batch workload plus scheduler bug; review found no significant direct contribution from AI-authored code but change volume outpaced verification controls; an automated dependency upgrade passed checks yet failed in production. Responses: monitoring, rollout safeguards, rollback capacity, scheduling automated changes in owning teams' working hours — [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/10/spotify-ai-quality-controls)
- Spotify: agents performed measurably worse in fragmented codebases; review load rose ~76% — [Spotify Eng](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/6/code-with-claude-coding-is-no-longer-the-constraint)
- Gartner (Jun 2025): >40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end-2027 due to cost, unclear value, risk controls; forecast, not coding-specific — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/artificial-intelligence/over-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-scrapped-by-2027-says-gartner)
- Deloitte Australia partial refund over AI-generated report errors (non-coding) — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/artificial-intelligence/deloitte-still-bets-on-ai-with-anthropic-deal-after-inaccurate-report-refund-saga)

### Inferences
- Budget a per-engineer token cap and cost dashboard before scaling; Uber learned it after the fact. Invest in review/verification capacity and rollback alongside generation capacity. Standardised, consistent codebases yield better agent results (Spotify), relevant for a consultancy with heterogeneous client repos.

### Gaps
- No primary documentation of reversals at consultancies. Kiro outage claim unverified. Cost-per-engineer baselines for non-Uber firms not found. Not covered: Copilot agent, Cursor, Codex, Devin enterprise failures with data; Pragmatic Engineer/The Information/Register pieces beyond those cited.
