# Consultancy / SI constraints and commercials for agent-delivered software (as of 2026-11-12)

Background research, not legal advice. Search-tool summaries were used for many items; primary documents were fetched only for the Anthropic terms, the Claude Code docs, and the Reuters-syndicated and HFS pieces. Items marked (secondary) need primary verification.

## Client consent and contracts (MSA/SOW, IP, indemnity, Anthropic terms)

### Takeaway
Anthropic's commercial terms give the SI (as customer) output ownership, no-training, and a capped-exempt IP indemnity with significant exclusions. Those exclusions (modifications, combination with other tech, customer inputs) map onto exactly what an SI does, so the SI cannot assume it can pass the indemnity through to clients unchanged. Contract practice is moving to short AI addenda covering disclosure, assignment and uncapped or separately capped AI-output IP indemnity, but sources are mostly practitioner blogs; I found no SI-specific MSA text.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic Commercial Terms (version metadata Jun 17, 2025), Section B: "Anthropic may not train models on Customer Content from Services" (Inputs and Outputs); Customer owns Outputs and Anthropic assigns any rights it has in them. (US-law contract; governing law not checked) — [Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
- Section K.1: Anthropic defends and pays judgments/settlements for third-party IP claims alleging that the Customer's paid use of the Services, or Outputs, infringes. Section K.3 exclusions: customer modifications to Services or Outputs; combination with technology/content Anthropic did not provide; Inputs or customer-provided data; use the customer knows or should know infringes; patented inventions practised in an Output; trademark claims from use of Output in commerce; also fraud, wilful misconduct, violations of law, breach of terms. — [Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
- Section L.3: liability capped at fees paid in the previous 12 months, excluding consequential damages, but the cap does not apply to the Section K indemnity. — [Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
- Claude Code docs: commercial users (Team, Enterprise, API, third-party platforms, Claude Gov) are not trained on, absent opt-in such as the Development Partner Program (first-party API only, not Bedrock/Vertex). Standard commercial retention is 30 days; Zero Data Retention is available to qualified accounts on Claude Code for Enterprise, per-organisation, not in the standard plan. Local transcripts are stored in plaintext in ~/.claude/projects for 30 days by default. — [Claude Code data usage](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage)
- Contract practice (US-centric practitioner commentary): add a short AI addendum to MSAs/engagement letters rather than rewriting; undisclosed AI use in deliverables may leave a firm uninsured if a claim arises; courts hold professionals responsible for AI errors; work-for-hire language assumes a human author; customers often want full assignment including AI-generated parts; vendors often carve AI outputs out of IP indemnity, and guidance suggests the indemnity expressly cover AI output and sit outside the cap; boilerplate SOW indemnity can make the SI indemnify the client for third-party IP claims. (secondary, vendor/blog level) — [Seller's playbook for MSAs/SOWs](https://stateofai.pages.dev/research/06-security-frontier/ai-client-facing-contracts-seller-playbook); [Bloomberg Law: what to negotiate before deploying AI coding assistants](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/what-to-negotiate-before-deploying-ai-coding-assistants-at-work); [Above the Law, Mar 2026](https://abovethelaw.com/2026/03/from-boilerplate-to-architecture-how-ai-broke-the-monolithic-ip-clause/)
- Copyright, US: Copyright Office Part 2 report (29 Jan 2025) requires human authorship; prompts alone do not suffice; protection possible for human-authored elements, creative selection/arrangement and modification; selection among outputs is not authorship. — [Akin summary](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/us-copyright-office-releases-part-2-of-ai-report); [Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/ai-report-part-2-copyrightability/)
- Copyright, UK: CDPA s9(3) gives computer-generated works to the person making the arrangements. UK Government report of 18 Mar 2026 (under Data (Use and Access) Act 2025) proposes removing it while protecting AI-assisted works with human creative choices; non-binding, no bill found. Commentators differ on how firm the repeal intent is ("may repeal"). — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/03/19/unfinished-works-uk-government-kicks-ai-copyright-reform-down-the-road-102mnep); [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/ip/2026-03/uk-government-report-on-copyright-and-ai-concludes-more-evidence-is-needed-although-s9-3-cdpa-could-go)

### Inferences
- Because AI-written code may have thin or no copyright in US/UK, an SI selling "ownership" should assign whatever rights exist, rely on contractual confidentiality/trade-secret protections, and avoid warranting copyrightability.
- Anthropic's K.3 exclusions (modification, combination, customer inputs) mean most real SI deliverables fall partly outside the upstream indemnity; any SI indemnity to clients is likely wider than what it receives (back-to-back gap). Enterprise negotiated paper may differ from the public terms; not checked.
- The indemnity is carved out of the cap, so Anthropic's exposure to the SI is uncapped for covered claims; the SI's own exposure to clients is a contract-by-contract question.

### Gaps
- No SI's actual MSA/SOW AI clauses, nor surveys of how many clients now require disclosure, were found. India and EU-specific rules on copyright in AI-generated code were not researched (EU: no source found).
- Anthropic's separate ZDR terms, DPA and any enterprise-negotiated indemnity changes not read in full. A third-party site reported "no retention" on API; contradicted by Anthropic's own 30-day standard retention above, so disregard it.

## Data and security (client code to provider, client tenancy, regulated and air-gapped clients, prompt injection, sandboxing)

### Takeaway
Claude Code can run against Bedrock, Vertex (now "Agent Platform") and Foundry with telemetry/feedback off by default, which is the main route to satisfying clients who forbid code leaving their tenancy; CI integrations (GitLab, GitHub Actions) use OIDC to the client's cloud. Prompt injection against coding agents in CI is a demonstrated, real supply-chain vector in 2026. I found nothing on true air-gapped use.

### Cited Findings
- Provider data flow: on Bedrock, Vertex Agent Platform, Foundry and Claude Platform on AWS, metrics, error reports and /feedback are default-off; session quality surveys and the WebFetch domain check (hostname sent to api.anthropic.com) still run unless disabled; encryption at rest: Bedrock AES-256 with optional KMS CMK, Vertex CMEK available; Foundry "Hosted on Azure" keeps prompts/completions within Azure with only usage metadata and safety-flagged content egressing to Anthropic. — [Claude Code data usage](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage)
- Claude Code supports self-hosted environments for sessions on customer infrastructure (details of what still reaches Anthropic are on a separate page, not read). — [Claude Code data usage](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage)
- GitLab CI/CD integration (beta, maintained by GitLab): runs on the customer's runners with branch protection and approvals; Bedrock via AWS OIDC role and Vertex via Workload Identity Federation; changes land as MRs. — [Claude Code GitLab CI/CD](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/gitlab-ci-cd)
- Prompt injection: "Clinejection" (early 2026): a malicious GitHub issue title hijacked an AI triage workflow (running Claude via GitHub Actions) with broad shell access and `allowed_non_write_users: "*"`, leading to an unauthorised npm release of cline@2.3.0. — [Snyk](https://snyk.io/blog/cline-supply-chain-attack-prompt-injection-github-actions); [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-clinejection-prompt-injection-cicd-cache-p/)
- Black Hat 2026 reporting: flaws in Anthropic, Google and OpenAI coding agents (prompt injection to RCE/credential theft; persistent injection via writable AGENTS.md); root cause described as hidden trust assumptions in agent harnesses. CVE identifiers conflict between sources (CSA cites CVE-2025-66032; the Black Hat write-up cites CVE-2026-54316), unresolved. — [eSecurityPlanet](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/threats/black-hat-2026-critical-flaws-found-in-anthropic-google-and-openai-coding-agents/); [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-claude-code-github-action-prompt-injection/)
- Anthropic certifications per its support page and trackers: ISO/IEC 42001:2023, ISO 27001:2022, SOC 2 Type I/II; HIPAA via configuration plus BAA (appears in only one language version of the page); Claude regional compliance page adds ISO 27017/27018 and CSA STAR; SOC 2 reports are request-gated at trust.anthropic.com. (secondary; currency of certificates not confirmed) — [Anthropic support: certifications](https://support.anthropic.com/id/articles/10015870-sertifikasi-apa-saja-yang-telah-diperoleh-anthropic); [regional compliance](https://claude.com/redirect/claudeai.v1.957c92e8-b936-4235-ab71-d9b7ae9f7af3/regional-compliance)
- US public sector: as of Oct 2026, reported that Claude Enterprise lacks FedRAMP authorisation and is pursuing Moderate via 20x; "Claude for Government" reported FedRAMP High; Bedrock GovCloud reported FedRAMP High and DoD IL4/IL5 (single weak source). FedRAMP published 2026 Consolidated Rules on 25 Jun 2026 and renamed levels to Class A/B/C in May 2026. (secondary) — [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/anthropic-claude-enterprise-fedramp-moderate-20x/); [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/07/fedramp-2026-consolidated-rules); [FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/fedramp-20x-widely-available-to-cloud-services-with-release-of-2026-consolidated-rules/)

### Inferences
- The SI's practical pattern is per-client configuration: the client's own cloud account and runners, OIDC (no static keys), nonessential traffic disabled, ZDR where on first-party API, and agents never triggered by untrusted input (issues/PRs from outsiders).
- Provider choice is a contract lever: Bedrock/Vertex/Foundry-hosted-on-Azure keep data in the client's existing DPA and sub-processor chain.

### Gaps
- No sources on air-gapped/on-prem model hosting, on banks' or health systems' actual AI-coding policies, on sandboxing standards expected by clients, or on slopsquatting. HIPAA BAA scope and Claude Gov details unverified.

## Regulation and standards (EU AI Act, ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF, SOC 2, DORA, FCA/PRA, SLSA, SBOM)

### Takeaway
The EU AI Act high-risk dates are being pushed back (provisional deal 7 May 2026: Annex III to 2 Dec 2027, product-embedded to 2 Aug 2028), and it mostly bites the SI where it builds or deploys high-risk systems for clients, not through using a coding agent per se. DORA is the sharpest sector rule for EU finance: AI services and their subcontractors fall into ICT third-party risk. No standard yet mandates marking AI-written code in provenance.

### Cited Findings
- EU AI Act (EU): Commission's Nov 2025 digital omnibus proposed moving high-risk obligations from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027; a provisional deal reported 7 May 2026 sets Annex III systems at 2 Dec 2027 and product-embedded at 2 Aug 2028; formal adoption status not confirmed in sources; GPAI rules have applied since Aug 2025 and Art. 50 transparency from Aug 2026. Sources conflict on whether registration of systems deemed not high-risk was removed or reinstated. — [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act); [IDTechWire](https://idtechwire.com/eu-moves-to-delay-high-risk-ai-act-compliance-to-december-2027/); [OneTrust](https://www.onetrust.com/blog/eu-digital-omnibus-proposes-delay-of-ai-compliance-deadlines/)
- DORA (EU finance): first 19 critical ICT third-party providers designated 18 Nov 2025 under direct ESA oversight; financial entities must keep a register of ICT contractual arrangements (Arts. 28-30); ESAs Joint Committee statement of 31 Jul 2026 on frontier-AI ICT risk; BaFin non-binding AI ICT-risk guidance; one commentator argues AI/MCP tools belong on the Art. 28 register (interpretation, not authority). — [Goodwin, Aug 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-fs-frontier-ai-dora-service-providers-to-eu-financial-entities-take-note); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102nbyc/lessons-from-the-european-supervisory-authorities-first-dora-major-incident-repo); [Peliqan](https://peliqan.io/blog/dora-mcp-financial-services/)
- Provenance (global/US-led): SLSA v1.2 (24 Nov 2025) has no AI-authorship category; CISA and G7 partners published minimum AI SBOM elements on 12 May 2026 (neither exhaustive nor mandatory); CycloneDX 1.6 has ML-BOM fields, "Agent BOM" unratified; proposals exist for per-commit generation records using in-toto/SLSA/Sigstore; Linux kernel assisted-by tags grew from 31 commits (7.0) to 1,111 (7.2) per one report. (secondary) — [TODO Group, 14 Jul 2026](https://todogroup.org/blog/2026-07-14-verifiable-provenance-ai-assisted-contributions/); [DZone](https://dzone.com/articles/verifiable-provenance-ai-code); [CSA note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-agents-software-supply-chain-risk-20260/)
- EU Cyber Resilience Act: SBOM requirement reported from 11 Dec 2027; reporting obligations already in force (48h detailed assessment per one source); Cloudsmith survey: only 25% of teams generate and verify SBOMs every build. (secondary) — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/it-infrastructure/news/366651862/AI-coding-tools-run-riot-over-SBOM-controls-as-EU-CRA-looms)

### Inferences
- AI-use disclosure and an audit trail (which model, which commit, which human approved) is the defensible baseline for DORA-regulated clients regardless of the absence of an AI-authorship standard.
- The SI's own ISO 42001/SOC 2 posture will matter as client questionnaires evolve; the SI inherits but cannot rely on Anthropic's certifications.

### Gaps
- Not researched or no source found: NIST AI RMF specifics; UK FCA/PRA (SS2/21 outsourcing, operational resilience) and UK AI rules; India (DPDP Act, RBI/SEBI); ISO 42001 certification for SIs; whether the EU AI Act treats AI coding assistants as in-scope for deployer obligations (no source addressed software development specifically); primary text of the ESA statement.

## Heterogeneity (one method across stacks, CI, source hosts, brownfield)

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. Claude Code is CLI/SDK-based so it is host-agnostic in principle, with documented first-class integrations for GitHub Actions and GitLab (beta, GitLab-maintained); per-repo CLAUDE.md carries each client's rules. I found no SI reporting on Azure DevOps, Bitbucket or brownfield approaches.

### Cited Findings
- GitLab integration is built on the Claude Code CLI and Agent SDK, uses project CLAUDE.md for standards, runs on the client's runners, and is flagged beta. — [Claude Code GitLab CI/CD](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/gitlab-ci-cd)
- Partner ecosystem: Anthropic's Claude Partner Network with SIs as deployment owners; Accenture (Dec 2025 multi-year deal, "tens of thousands" of developers on Claude Code), Cognizant (Claude to up to 350,000 staff, Global Premier Partner, 30,000+ trained), Deloitte (reported 470,000 staff access). Headcounts vary across sources; one commentator says access has run about ten times ahead of completed training. — [Anthropic-Accenture](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-accenture-partnership); [eWeek on Cognizant](https://www.eweek.com/news/anthropic-cognizant-deal/); [Avasant](https://avasant.com/report/anthropic-gains-enterprise-momentum-through-expanding-partner-engagements/); [Horses for Sources](https://www.horsesforsources.com/anthropic-is-devouring-it-services_041326/)

### Inferences
- A workable pattern is a thin common core (method, evidence format) plus a per-client adapter layer (host, CI, cloud provider, rules in CLAUDE.md and hooks); the sources support the building blocks, not any SI's proven implementation.

### Gaps
- No sources on Azure DevOps/Bitbucket support, mainframe/legacy (COBOL) agent work, or SI "adapter" architectures. Search did not reach this.

## Commercial model (billing, pricing, discounts, margin, token cost)

### Takeaway
Clients are demanding AI productivity be passed through: reported 25-30% price cuts requested, mid-contract renegotiation within 24 months, and a shift from FTE/rate cards to outcome, fixed-price and gain-share. Large Indian SIs have grown only 1-3% while some mid-tier firms grew double digits. Token cost as cost of delivery was not found quantified in any source.

### Cited Findings
- TCS CEO: about 80% of contracts in finance/HR/other business services are outcome-based (roughly doubled since late 2023, per a source); Cognizant-Daimler Truck deal (Feb 2026) splits AI cost savings between vendor and client; HCLTech-E.ON (June 2025) pays nothing in year one then depends on efficiency gains; Persistent's CEO says clients want the same work for 25-30% less; Tech Mahindra's CEO says some rivals assume 70-80% productivity gains over 5-7 years and guarantee prices despite rising chip costs; Infosys walked away from uneconomic contracts; contracts getting shorter; Nifty IT down about a fifth in 2026; TCS/Infosys/Wipro/HCLTech grew 1-3% vs Persistent +16% (Apr-Jun) and Coforge up a third. (India/global, Reuters-syndicated, Aug 2026) — [The Star/Reuters, 21 Aug 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/08/21/ai-reshapes-india039s-it-services-sector-contracts-as-clients-demand-more-for-less)
- HFS Research: clients reopening contracts within 24 months of signing; pressure highest above $50M ACV, on pandemic-era annuity deals; pricing moving from FTEs/rate cards/offshore ratios to productivity commitments, outcome pricing, gain-share; no survey base disclosed (interview-based; Fersht, NelsonHall's Parab). — [HFS Research](https://www.hfsresearch.com/news/ai-drives-it-contract-renegotiations-within-24-months-as-pricing-models-shift/)
- Deflation estimates diverge: TCS CEO 5-8% industry deflation over 12 months; HCLTech CEO about 2-3% a year (up to 5% in one report). (secondary) — [Analytics India Mag](https://analyticsindiamag.com/it-services/ai-deflation-is-beginning-to-hollow-out-indias-it-revenue-engine)
- Accenture FY26 Q4 (reported ~1 Oct 2026): revenue $18.7B (+6% USD, +7% LC), bookings $22.2B (FY $84.5B), 400+ clients started advanced AI projects in FY26, nearly 110,000 AI/data professionals; FY27 guidance implies about 1-3.5% organic growth; management says falling token prices unlock more use cases; some analysts flag pricing deflation. Stock move reports conflict. (secondary) — [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/accentures-q4-results-indicate-enterprise-ai-broadening); [National CIO Review](https://nationalcioreview.com/articles-insights/cio-field-notes/accenture-sees-lower-ai-costs-unlocking-more-enterprise-use-cases/)
- Globant Q2 2026 (14 Aug): Glob.AI ARR $52.8M (from $32.8M in March), exit-2026 target at least $110M; AI Pods about 2% of revenue, 45 clients, management says gross margin about 10 points above traditional services (unaudited management claim); adjusted operating margin 13.2%; $32.3M optimisation charge; FY26 revenue guidance cut to $2.428-2.462B; sells on output/consumption rather than hours. — [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/globant-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-14/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/globant-glob-ai-arr-jumps-61-in-one-quarter-to-52-8-million/)
- Reuters-syndicated report quotes Everest's Jimit Arora that odds are "very much in favour of clients". — [The Star/Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/08/21/ai-reshapes-india039s-it-services-sector-contracts-as-clients-demand-more-for-less)

### Inferences
- The price-to-cost spread is the SI's margin only if the SI keeps part of the productivity gain; fixed-price/outcome pricing moves delivery-variance and token-cost risk from client to SI, and Tech Mahindra's remark suggests rising compute cost is a recognised risk.
- Accenture's disclosure (growth guidance low single digit despite record bookings) is consistent with deflation offsetting volume, but no source connects it explicitly.

### Gaps
- No usable data on: Infosys, Capgemini, Thoughtworks pricing; Cognizant specifics beyond the Daimler deal; token spend as a percentage of delivery cost; margin effects at Accenture/Infosys/TCS; Gartner/ISG/Everest primary reports (paywalled, only quoted second-hand).

## Workforce (pyramid, junior roles, offshore)

### Takeaway
Indian IT headcount and fresher intake are falling and commentators (including a former Infosys CFO) say the classic pyramid is "gone", though companies attribute layoffs to delivery-model changes and some peers still hire freshers; figures conflict between sources.

### Cited Findings
- Former Infosys CFO V. Balakrishnan says the traditional pyramid is "gone" because coding agents reduce the need for entry-level engineers. — [The Star/Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/08/21/ai-reshapes-india039s-it-services-sector-contracts-as-clients-demand-more-for-less)
- TCS reportedly shed 23,460 employees in FY26; TCS attributes it to delivery-model changes, not AI; fresher plans reported as about 25,000 vs about 40,000 for FY27 (conflict, unresolved); a Bloomberg-sourced June 2026 piece says TCS will curb hiring as it scales AI agents; Wipro cut its fresher target to 7,500-8,000; Infosys expected to keep about 20,000; top IT firms' headcount fell by over 7,000 in FY26; Xpheno: entry-level openings down 44% YoY. (India; mostly secondary) — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/in-depth/tcs-layoffs-signal-collapse-of-it-sectors-traditional-talent-pyramid); [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/recruiting-and-onboarding/tcs-makes-25000-fresher-offers-says-changing-delivery-modelsnot-ailed-to-layoffs-49194); [IANS](https://ianslive.in/top-it-firms-hiring-turns-negative-as-headcount-falls-over-7000-in-fy26--20260425104000); [YourStory, Sep 2026](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/indias-it-services-pyramid-is-hollowing-out-from-the-middle)
- Accenture reportedly reports no AI-driven headcount reduction and is upskilling plus hiring data/AI roles. (secondary) — [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/accentures-q4-results-indicate-enterprise-ai-broadening)

### Inferences
- Reports of "adding at the bottom, cutting from the middle" (YourStory) and the structural-not-cyclical thesis are analyst framing, not measured outcomes.

### Gaps
- No primary data on reviewer/verification capacity, junior apprenticeship models, offshore-onshore ratio changes, or Capgemini/Cognizant/Globant headcount. Figures for Cognizant and Infosys hiring not verified.
